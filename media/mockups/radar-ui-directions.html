Radar — visual direction study

Two audiences,
one house.

Radar talks to a frightened resident on a wrist and a phone, and to an investigator at a desk afterward. This study gives each surface its own register — soft and forgiving for the person living through it, dark and evidentiary for the record they left behind — carried by the mascot in a different pose on every screen, doing a different job each time.

direction B — a second exploration

Same house, an ambient reading of it.

A second system, from the meditation-app reference: a soft dusk gradient standing in for the calm-app's dawn one, dark frosted glass standing in for its white cards, and a rounded, friendly Manrope in place of Fraunces and Inter. The 24-hour mood dial becomes a quiet-hours dial; the session list becomes the camera's recent clips. Same two screens as "Home" and "Replay" above, restated in a softer, more ambient key — useful to hold up against the darker evidentiary direction and see which one the room responds to.
