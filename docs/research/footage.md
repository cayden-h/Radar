# Footage sources for the demo video

Researched 2026-09-19.

The video is cut at home from our own material. This file covers the **B-roll** we do not shoot ourselves: fire behavior, fireground, responder context.

**Read the licensing rules first. A copyright strike on the Devpost video is an unrecoverable failure on submission day.**

---

## Licensing rules

1. **Public domain or explicit open license only.** No exceptions, no "it was on YouTube."
2. **Credit everything on screen or in the description**, even where not legally required. It costs nothing and it reads as rigor.
3. **When the license is unclear, do not use it.** There is no time on submission day to argue about it.
4. **Never use real incident footage showing identifiable victims.** Separate from copyright, this is a dignity issue on a project about people dying at home, and a judge will notice.

---

## Tier 1: US federal, public domain

### NIST Fire Research Division

**Best source available.** NIST is a US federal agency, so its works are public domain under the federal government exception.
NIST's own policy: information on NIST sites "may be distributed or copied," and "use of appropriate byline/photo/image credits is requested."
Note the caveat in their policy: individual products may carry their own disclaimers, so check the page you pull from.

Source: [NIST Copyright Policy](https://www.nist.gov/oism/copyrights)

Collections worth pulling from:

| Collection | What it is |
|---|---|
| [Fire.Gov portal](https://www.nist.gov/el/fire-research-division-73300/firegov-fire-service) | Hub for fire service research video and data |
| [Fire Calorimetry Database supercut](https://www.nist.gov/video/fire-calorimetry-database-supercut) | Downloadable experiment footage with heat release rate overlaid |
| [Governors Island wind-driven fire experiments](https://www.nist.gov/el/fire-research-division-73300/firegov-fire-service/videos-wind-driven-fires-governors-island) | Full-scale experiments run with FDNY |
| [PPV experiment videos](https://www.nist.gov/el/fire-research-division-73300/firegov-fire-service/ppv-experiment-videos) | Eight simultaneous camera angles per experiment |
| [NFRL featured videos](https://www.nist.gov/el/fire-research-division-73300/national-fire-research-laboratory-73306/videos) | National Fire Research Laboratory |
| [Fire Studies](https://www.nist.gov/el/fire-research-division-73300/firegov-fire-service/fire-studies) | Downloadable experimental fire data and video |

**The shot we specifically want:** a room going from ordinary to unsurvivable in under three minutes.
NIST's legacy-versus-modern furnishing comparisons show exactly that, and it is the visual proof of the "unconscious before you know" claim in `incidents.md`.
That single clip does more pitch work than any amount of generic flame B-roll.

### USFA / FEMA

[usfa.fema.gov](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/statistics/) - also federal, also public domain. Photos, statistics graphics, some video.
Good for data-backed title cards.

## Tier 2: check the license every time

### UL FSRI (Fire Safety Research Institute)

[fsri.org](https://fsri.org/) has excellent modern fire dynamics research, much of it done jointly with NIST and FDNY.

**UL Research Institutes is a private nonprofit, not a government agency, so its material is NOT automatically public domain.**
Where a piece is co-published with NIST, the NIST copy is the safe one to take. Otherwise read their terms or skip it.

### Stock libraries

Pexels and Pixabay carry usable fire and emergency B-roll under permissive licenses.
**Check the license on each individual asset**; the site-wide terms are not a guarantee for every file.

---

## Do not use

- **Firefighter helmet-cam and bodycam clips off YouTube.** Almost always copyrighted by the uploader or the department. Some US department footage is a public record in some states, but the analysis is state-specific and it is not worth the risk the night before submission.
- **News broadcast footage.** Always rights-managed.
- **Anything showing a real identifiable victim or a real address.**
- **Real 911 call audio.** Recordings exist publicly, but using a real emergency where a real person was dying, as texture in a hackathon demo, is the wrong call. Our own ElevenLabs call is better material anyway and we control it completely.

## What we shoot ourselves

Most of the video is ours. See `media/CLAUDE.md` for the shot list.

- The house, the rooms, the walls the system sees through.
- The staged fall.
- The Pi and router in place.
- The agent console, screen-recorded separately and cut in. Do not film a laptop screen.
- The phone, showing the transcript and guidance arriving.

**External B-roll is seasoning, not substance.** The reason to include any is to make the stakes legible in the first fifteen seconds.
Budget a handful of seconds total, keep it, and move on to the thing we actually built.
